## Key Data Points (Standardize Across Sessions)

### Prevalence Statistics
- **25%** of today's 20-year-olds will become disabled before retirement (SSA actuarial tables)
- **90%** of disabilities from illness, not accidents (CDIA)
- **51M+** working adults lack DI beyond basic Social Security
- **65%** of private sector has NO employer LTD
- **Average duration** when claim exceeds 90 days: **2.1-3.2 years**

### SSDI Facts
- **Average monthly benefit:** $1,582 (2025)
- **Maximum monthly benefit:** $4,018 (2025)
- **Initial denial rate:** 62%
- **Hearing approval rate:** 51-58%
- **Overall approval (all stages):** ~40-45%
- **Processing time:** 3-6 months initial, 12-18 months hearing (total 18-36 months)
- **SGA threshold:** $1,620/month non-blind (2025)
- **Earnings credits needed:** 40 credits, 20 in last 10 years for workers under 31

### Cost Benchmarks
- **Individual DI cost:** 2-5% of gross income (typical 3% for young healthy, 5% for older/rated)
- **$5,000/mo benefit at age 24:** ~$943/yr (Policygenius)
- **$5,000/mo benefit for physician at 30:** ~$1,100/yr
- **Physician residency pricing:** $150-$350/mo level premium
- **Attending physician pricing:** $150-$600/mo depending on specialty, benefit amount, riders

### 2026 SSDI Formula (Bend Points)
```
AIME = Σ(top_35_years_indexed_earnings) / (35 × 12)
PIA  = 0.90 × min(AIME, 1286) + 0.32 × max(0, min(AIME, 7749) - 1286) + 0.15 × max(0, AIME - 7749)
```

## Occupation Classification System

| Class | Examples | Own-Occ Eligibility | Typical Max Monthly Benefit |
|-------|----------|-------------------|---------------------------|

| **3A** | Executives, admin professionals, software engineers, white-collar | ✅ Own-occ | $10,000-$15,000 |

## Essential Riders (for comprehensive analysis)

| Rider | Cost Impact | Priority | When Essential |

## Key Tax Rules (IRC)

| Premium Payer | Premium Tax Treatment | Benefit Tax Treatment | Planning Implication |
|--------------|---------------------|---------------------|---------------------|
| Employer (group LTD) | Not taxable to employee (IRC §106) | Taxable as ordinary income (IRC §105(a)) | $15K/mo group benefit @37% = $9,450 net |
| Individual (after-tax) | Not deductible | Tax-free (IRC §104(a)(3)) | Full benefit amount retained |
| Self-employed (deducted) | Deductible on Schedule C | Taxable | Self-employed must choose: deduct now or tax-free later |
| S-corp (paid through business) | Deductible by business | Taxable to owner | Same trade-off as self-employed |

**Net benefit multiplier:** The same $10K/month benefit is worth $10K (individual after-tax) vs $6,300 (employer-paid @37% bracket). A 59% difference.

## Common Research Blinds Spots (check each session)

1. **Group-to-Individual substitution gap** — Most clients think "I have coverage through work" without understanding the differences in tax treatment, definition, portability, and benefit caps. Always model both scenarios.
2. **SSDI as uncertain income** — Never model SSDI as guaranteed. Use 40% probability-adjusted factor and flag denial risk.
3. **State regulation variance** — NY, CA, FL, TX, MA have the most regulation. Carrier offerings vary by state. Always check client's state before making specific recommendations.
4. **Pre-existing condition risk** — Standard health class is aspirational. Always show Preferred/Standard/Rated tiers.
5. **Business overhead expense gap** — Standard DI doesn't cover practice overhead. Business owners need separate BOE policy.
6. **Mental/nervous limitations** — Many policies cap mental health claims at 24 months. This is especially important for certain specialties (anesthesiology, ER).
7. **Non-cancelable vs guaranteed renewable** — Non-cancelable locks BOTH premiums AND coverage cancellability. Guaranteed renewable only locks coverage (premiums can adjust by class). Big difference in long-term cost stability.
