# Value of Optimal Decumulation — Quantification Methodology Reference

## When to Load

Load this reference when research encounters competitors making quantified value claims about retirement income optimization ($X saved, N more years of retirement, Y% improvement in after-tax income), or when planning WealthForge's own quantified value proposition for investor narrative or advisor sales.

## The Three Value Claim Archetypes

Every competing retirement distribution tool uses one of three metric archetypes:

| Archetype | Example | Used By | Best For |
|-----------|---------|---------|----------|
| **Extra Years of Retirement Income** | "7 more years" or "$1M" | Income Solver (T. Rowe Price) | Client-facing communication, intuitive ("how long will my money last?") |
| **Lifetime Tax Dollars Saved** | "$625K-$849K" / "37% reduction" | Mezzi | Marketing headlines, aspirational gap creation |
| **Percentage Improvement (multi-metric)** | "33% more income, 40% lower taxes, 45% more bequests" | SEI LifeYield (EY-validated) | Advisor sales, institutional credibility, most defensible |

**Recommendation:** The three-metric model (income + taxes + bequests) is the most defensible because it shows optimization across all dimensions with independent validation. WealthForge should adopt this model.

## The Baseline Strategy Problem

The single biggest driver of all value claim magnitudes is the **baseline strategy chosen for comparison** — not the optimizer's sophistication.

| Baseline | Magnitude of Improvement | Who Uses It |
|----------|------------------------|-------------|
| Naive/RMD-only withdrawals | $625K-$849K lifetime (largest headline) | Mezzi |
| "Conventional wisdom" (unspecified) | 7 extra years / $1M | Income Solver |
| Uncoordinated account-by-account management | 33%/40%/45% improvement | LifeYield/EY |
| Reasonable advisor practice | Much smaller (~2-5% of portfolio value) | No one publishes this — but it's the honest comparison |

**WealthForge implication:** Test against multiple baselines and be transparent about which is used. The strongest claims compare against "the average advisor practice" AND "a DIY retiree."

## Competitor Methodology Summaries

### Income Solver (Retiree Inc. / T. Rowe Price, Jan 2026)
- **Sample:** 5,700+ households from an affiliated retail advisory service
- **Wealth tiers:** $500K (10th %ile), $2.25M (50th %ile, main focus), $4.5M (90th %ile)
- **Assumptions:** Moderate market returns, 60/40 stock-bond across all accounts, appropriate SS PIA and spending per band
- **Most transparent disclosure** of any competitor — methodology published in offer guide PDF
- **Pricing:** $1,900-$5,000/yr single practitioner
- **Weakness:** Compares against "conventional wisdom" not a rigorous baseline

### Mezzi (StockTalk Inc.)
- **Method:** Cumulative lifetime tax projection over ~30-year retirement comparing naive vs. optimized withdrawal sequencing
- **The $625K-$849K** includes ALL tax interaction effects: RMD tax bombs, SS taxation, IRMAA, bracket creep
- **True marginal value** over a basic taxable-first strategy is probably 2-5% of lifetime portfolio value
- **Pricing disclaimer:** "Based on estimated annual savings for a portfolio of $1M or more"
- **Weakness:** Most aggressive assumptions; lumps effects already understood by any competent planner

### LifeYield/EY (now SEI LifeYield)
- **EY validation** provides the most credible independent verification
- **Multiple metrics:** income improvement + tax reduction + bequest increase
- **Morningstar finding:** 52 bps annual improvement from optimal asset location alone
- **Taxficient Score (0-100):** Industry standard — avg 52/100, optimized 70+
- **Strength:** Most defensible because multiple metrics show optimization across all dimensions

## Academic Foundation

### Withdrawal Efficiency Rate (WER) — Blanchett, Kowara, Chen (Morningstar 2012)
- WER = actual withdrawals ÷ theoretical maximum with perfect foresight
- Typical values: 50-80% — even best strategies achieve only 50-80% of theoretical max
- Five strategies compared: Constant Dollar (4% rule), Constant Percentage, 1/N (RMD), Dynamic Formula, Mortality Updating Failure Percentage (MUF)
- WER is the right **internal** benchmark but not suitable for marketing

### Blanchett & Finke (2025) — "Retirees Spend Lifetime Income, Not Savings"
- 7,498 observations across 10 HRS waves
- Retirees spend ~85% of lifetime income but only ~50% of non-annuitized savings
- Observed withdrawal rate from savings: ~2.1% for 65 married households (far below 4% rule)
- Implication: the right objective function may be "enable optimal spending behavior" not "maximize wealth"

## Five Common Accounting Challenges

1. **Baseline choice** — biggest single driver of headline number; always verify
2. **Return assumptions** — all tools use deterministic returns; none does stochastic optimization
3. **Spending assumptions** — "extra years" highly sensitive to assumed annual spending
4. **Household complexity** — simpler tools model individuals; sophisticated tools model households (2-3x more value)
5. **Horizon & discount rate** — 25yr vs 35yr horizon dramatically changes present-value calculations
