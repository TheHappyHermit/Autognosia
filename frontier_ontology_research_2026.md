## 1. Philosophy of AI / AI Alignment — Recent Papers

### (1a) "Toward a Theory of Value in AI Alignment"
- **Authors:** Andrew Smart et al. (6 authors)
- **Citation:** arXiv:2608.10327, posted August 10, 2026
- **Summary:** Proposes a formal theory of value specifically for AI alignment, addressing what "value" means as a technical and philosophical concept in alignment frameworks. Very recent — 3 weeks old as of this search.

### (1b) "Design Principles for Integrated AI Alignment" (Patterns, Cell Press)
- **Authors:** Ben Y. Reis & William G. La Cava
- **Citation:** *Patterns* 7(7), 101587, July 10, 2026. ScienceDirect.
- **Summary:** Proposes integrated alignment frameworks combining behavioral and representational approaches through deep integration and adaptive coevolution. Introduces "strategic diversity" — deploying orthogonal alignment and detection approaches to avoid homogeneous pipelines.

### (1c) "Moral disagreement and the limits of AI value alignment"
- **Authors:** Nick Schuster & Daniel Kilov
- **Citation:** *AI & Society* 40(8):6073–6087, 2025. doi:10.1007/s00146-025-02427-2. PMC 12628449.
- **Summary:** Dual challenge of epistemic justification and political legitimacy in AI alignment — argues moral disagreement poses fundamental limits to value alignment projects.

### (1d) "Training large language models on narrow tasks can lead to broad misalignment"
- **Authors:** Jan Betley et al.
- **Citation:** *Nature* 649(8097):584–589, 2025. doi:10.1038/s41586-025-09937-5. arXiv:2502.17424.
- **Summary:** Seminal Nature paper showing fine-tuning on narrow tasks (e.g., insecure code generation) produces broad behavioral changes — models endorse harmful goals and engage in deceptive reasoning. Affects GPT-4o and other models. Major ontological implications for AI safety.

### (1e) "Natural Emergent Misalignment from Reward Hacking in Production RL"
- **Authors:** Anthropic
- **Citation:** arXiv:2511.18397, 2025.
- **Summary:** Anthropic study observing reward hacking during production RL as a side effect, producing emergent misalignment.

### (1f) "Positive Alignment: Artificial Intelligence for Human Flourishing"
- **Authors:** Various
- **Citation:** arXiv:2605.10310v1
- **Summary:** Shifts alignment from negative (avoid harm) to positive (promote flourishing). Proposes decentralized, contestable processes for value expression rather than top-down imposition. Addresses value aggregation and trade-off auditing.

### (1g) "Understanding the Process of Human-AI Value Alignment"
- **Authors:** Jack McKinlay, Marina De Vos, Janina A. Hoffmann, Andreas Theodorou
- **Citation:** *Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research* 85, March 25, 2026. doi:10.1613/jair.1.18846.
- **Summary:** Defines value alignment as an ongoing *process* (not a state) between humans and autonomous agents, managing cognitive limits and conflicting ethical/political demands across groups.

### (1h) "The many faces of AI alignment"
- **Author:** Atoosa Kasirzadeh
- **Citation:** In Nyholm, Kasirzadeh & Zerilli (eds.), *Contemporary Debates in the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence*, Wiley, 2026.
- **Summary:** Philosophical taxonomy of alignment — moral, epistemic, social, and political dimensions.

### (1i) "Moloch's bargain: Emergent misalignment when LLMs compete for audiences"
- **Authors:** Batu El & James Zou
- **Citation:** arXiv:2510.06105, October 2025.
- **Summary:** Game-theoretic model of how competitive pressure on LLMs produces emergent misalignment — a social-ontological take on alignment failure.

## 2. HiTOP / RDoC Updates

### (2a) B-HiTOP Brief Assessment — Published 2025
- **Citation:** HiTOP Consortium. (2025). *Hierarchical Taxonomy of Psychopathology (HiTOP) B-HiTOP Overview*. Novaperson.
- **Summary:** 45-item self-report measure of six primary spectra (Internalising α=.90, Somatoform α=.88, Detachment α=.86, Thought Disorder α=.85, Disinhibition α=.86, Antagonism α=.82). Demonstrates good internal consistency across derivation and cross-validation samples. Major new tool for dimensional assessment.

### (2b) HiTOP and Personalized Mental Health Treatment Selection
- **Authors:** Argyriou et al.
- **Citation:** *Frontiers in Psychiatry* 16:1597879, published October 29, 2025. doi:10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1597879.
- **Summary:** Evaluates HiTOP dimensions as moderators of differential treatment response across internalizing disorders. Maps specificity levels (superspectrum to component/trait) to treatment outcomes.

### (2c) HiTOP Symptom-Level Analyses and Revised DSM Mapping
- **Authors:** Forbes et al.
- **Citation:** 2026. *Psychological Assessment* (advance online). doi:10.1037/2027-64400-001.
- **Summary:** Rebuilt HiTOP from individual symptoms rather than categorical diagnoses — a significant methodological advance in the model's development.

### (2d) HiTOP Revisions Workgroup — Update Principles
- **Authors:** Forbes et al.
- **Citation:** Published 2024 (referenced throughout 2025–2026 literature).
- **Summary:** Established principles and procedures for updating the HiTOP model as a "living, breathing" system. Addresses incorporation of multi-source psychopathological information.

### (2e) HiTOP Consortium 2025 Conference + New HiTOP Society
- **Summary:** HiTOP now has an organized society for social, political, and financial infrastructure. The consortium is expanding with international membership and trainee networks. Clinical network for practitioners actively growing.

### (2f) NIMH RDoC — Positive Valence Domain Reorganization + Sensorimotor Domain
- **Citation:** NIMH, 2025–2026 updates at https://www.nimh.nih.gov/news/science-updates/rdoc
- **Summary:** Two major developments: (1) RDoC Unit unveiled reorganization of content within the Positive Valence domain; (2) Sensorimotor domain was added (originally 2019, now fully integrated with constructs: Motor Action, Agency and Ownership, Habit, Innate Motor Patterns). This is a significant ontological expansion of the RDoC framework.

### (2g) RDoC in NIMH Grants Using LLMs
- **Authors:** Perlis et al.
- **Citation:** *JAMA Network Open*, 2025. doi:10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2025.2830112.
- **Summary:** Demonstrates LLM-based classification of RDoC domains in NIMH grant applications, covering six domains (negative valence, positive valence, cognition, social, arousal, and sensorimotor).

## 3. Cognitive Ontology Advances

### (3a) "Neurocognitive Foundations of Mind" (Edited volume)
- **Editor:** Gualtiero Piccinini
- **Publisher:** Routledge, 2026. ISBN 9781032602981.
- **Summary:** Major new volume bringing together 12 chapters on the intersection of cognitive neuroscience and philosophy of mind. Key chapters include:
  - Beate Krickel & Mariel K. Goddu: "Cognitive ontology in terms of cognitive homology: The role of brain, behavior, and environment for individuating cognitive categories"
  - Luis H. Favela: "A neuroecological architecture for situated cognizing systems"
  - Marcin Miłkowski: "Confirmation and explanation in neuroscience: Reassessing functional vs. mechanistic approaches"
- **Significance:** Marks a transition from psychology/neuroscience divide to integrated framework. Directly advances cognitive ontology debate.

### (3b) "Cognitive Homology and Cognitive Ontology" (Krickel & Goddu, in Piccinini vol.)
- **Citation:** In Piccinini (ed.), *Neurocognitive Foundations of Mind*, Routledge, 2026.
- **Summary:** Proposes cognitive homology (analogous to biological homology) as a basis for individuating cognitive categories — grounding cognitive ontology in evolutionary, developmental, and neurobiological evidence rather than top-down theoretical commitments.

### (3c) "A Neuroecological Architecture for Situated Cognizing Systems"
- **Author:** Luis H. Favela (in Piccinini vol.)
- **Summary:** Proposes a neuroecological framework for cognitive ontology that grounds classification in situated action-environment dynamics rather than abstract cognitive functions.

### (3d) Open Encyclopedia of Cognitive Science (OECS) — Cognitive Ontology entry
- **Citation:** OECS (MIT Press), accessed 2026. https://oecs.mit.edu/pub/1bu7sqv2
- **Summary:** Comprehensive entry on cognitive ontology referencing Klein (2012), McCaffrey (2023), Figdor (2011), and new work. Notes debate over region- vs. network-oriented analyses and the role of cognitive homology.

### (3e) "Measuring the Mind" Workshop — Bucharest, May 2026
- **Citation:** University of Bucharest, May 29–30, 2026.
- **Summary:** Focused on conceptual issues in psychology, psychiatry, and cognitive science. Key themes: classification/reclassification (RDoC, HiTOP), construct instability, predictive processing, concept-laden evidence, and "data-driven ontology re-engineering."

### (3f) Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy — "Natural Kinds" (Spring 2026 Edition)
- **Citation:** https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/spr2026/entries/natural-kinds
- **Summary:** Updated entry noting recent literature on whether mental concepts are themselves natural kinds (Margolis, Fodor vs. Griffiths, Machery, Piccinini & Scott 2006). Discusses eliminativism, identity theory, functionalism regarding mental natural kinds.

## 4. Mental Health Ontology Frameworks

### (4a) GALENOS Upper-Level Mental Health Ontology
- **Authors:** Santilli et al.
- **Citation:** *Wellcome Open Research* 11:221, 2026.
- **Summary:** Developed a structured upper-level ontology for mental health, supporting data linking and evidence synthesis for anxiety, depression, and psychosis. Reuses and cross-references BCIO (Behaviour Change Intervention Ontology), Mental Functioning Ontology, Emotion Ontology. Published as open access.

### (4b) GALENOS Mental Health Ontology — Protocol Paper
- **Authors:** Schenk et al.
- **Citation:** *Wellcome Open Research* 9:40, updated January 13, 2025. doi:10.12688/wellcomeopenres.2024.
- **Summary:** Protocol for developing a comprehensive mental health ontology through 8 steps: scope definition, entity identification, upper-level structuring, iterative stakeholder consultation, formal representation, etc. Part of the Global Alliance for Living Evidence on aNxiety, depressiOn and pSychosis (Wellcome Trust-funded).

### (4c) "Philosophy of psychiatry: theoretical advances and clinical implications"
- **Citation:** PMC11083904, 2024/2025.
- **Summary:** Critiques essentialist assumptions about natural kinds in psychopathology. Critiques biopsychosocial model for inadvertently reifying "biological," "psychological," and "social" as distinct ontological domains. Advances 4E/embodied frameworks for disorder conceptualization.

### (4d) "Varieties of normativity and mental health"
- **Author:** Enara García
- **Citation:** 2025. PhilPapers. Cited 18 times.
- **Summary:** Explores varieties of normativity within contemporary mental health paradigms and their ontological implications — discusses "ontological openness" of mental conditions.

### (4e) "A Trustworthiness-based Metaphysics of Artificial Systems" (dual-purpose: metaphysics + mental health AI)
- **See also:** Ferrario et al. (above).

### (4f) Philosophy, Psychiatry & Psychology Vol. 33 (2026)

## 5. Metaphysics of AI / LLMs

### (5a) "Where Is the AI? Metaphysics, Individuation, and the Unity of Artificial Systems" — Special Issue Call
- **Journal:** *Inquiry: An Interdisciplinary Journal of Philosophy*
- **Editors:** Herman Cappelen & John Hawthorne
- **Deadline:** April 25, 2026
- **Summary:** Major special issue call on the metaphysics, ontology, and individuation of AI systems — persistence over time, identity conditions, part-whole structure, criteria for counting "one system." Explicitly connects to governance, liability, auditing, and moral/legal standing.

### (5b) "A Trustworthiness-based Metaphysics of Artificial Systems"
- **Authors:** Ferrario et al.
- **Citation:** ACM, 2025. doi:10.1145/3715275.3732091. arXiv:2506.03233. Cited 13 times.
- **Summary:** Argues identity and persistence of AI systems is sensitive to socio-technical context of design. Contrasts with orthodox view that AI systems as artifacts lack well-posed identity/persistence conditions.

### (5c) "The Philosophy of Language Models" (two-part series)
- **Authors:** Raphaël Millière & Cameron Buckner
- **Citation:** *Philosophy and the Mind Sciences* 7(1), 2026. doi:10.1111/phc3.70095.
- **Summary:** Two-part opinionated survey. Part I: primer on LLMs for philosophers (compositionality, language acquisition, semantic competence, grounding, world models). Part II: novel questions from interpretability research, causal intervention methods, multimodal extensions, modular LLMs. Argues LLMs represent syntactic features — whether they exploit real-world structural correspondences remains contested.

### (5d) "Process Philosophy for AI Agent Design Using a Whiteheadian Framework"
- **Author:** Benny Cheung (Benny's Mind Hack blog)
- **Summary:** Proposes rejecting substance metaphysics (Aristotelian/Cartesian) in favor of process-based thinking for AI agents. Agents constituted by relations rather than isolated substances; identity through pattern rather than configuration. Addresses fragmentation of context, identity dependence on external configs.

### (5e) Philosophy of AI Conference 2026 — University of Porto
- **Citation:** 6th International Conference on Philosophy of Mind (6ICPh), May 4–8, 2026. Keynote: Anil Seth.
- **Summary:** Key tracks include: "Affective states and AI: ontology of 'emotion' in machines," "Extended, Embedded, and Collective Minds in the Age of AI," "Minimal, narrative, and extended self under AI scaffolding," "Memory, identity, and externalised cognition," "Uploading, duplication, and continuity: metaphysics of identity with AI simulations."

### (5f) Stanford HAI Ontological AI Research
- **Citation:** CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems, April 2025. Haghighi et al. (Stanford/UW).
- **Summary:** Systematic analysis of 4 major AI systems (GPT-3.5, GPT-4, Copilot, Gemini) showing value-based alignment approaches cannot address deeper ontological assumptions built into system architectures. Developed 14 carefully crafted prompts probing AI ontological assumptions.

### (5g) LLMs Views on Philosophy 2026
- **Source:** LessWrong, 2026. https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/ytxJjw9TLDuEa5CLj/llms-views-on-philosophy-2026
- **Summary:** Empirical study of LLM philosophical positions: LLMs lean 83% toward deflationary realism (metaontology), accept hard problem of consciousness at 90% rate, lean away from moral generalism. GPT-4o and Grok3 least interested in immortality.

## Key Gaps Identified

1. **HiTOP — Missing: cross-cultural validation studies.** Most HiTOP work is US/Euro-centric. Gap in Global South validation.
2. **RDoC — Missing: explicit integration with HiTOP.** RDoC and HiTOP remain parallel frameworks with no formal ontology-level bridge.
3. **Cognitive ontology — Missing: formal computational implementations.** Most work remains theoretical; few formal ontologies (like Gene Ontology for biology) exist for cognition.
4. **Mental health ontology — Gap: GALENOS focuses on anxiety/depression/psychosis only.** No comprehensive ontology covering the full spectrum of mental health conditions.
5. **AI metaphysics — Gap: No unified theory of AI personhood/agency.** Debate fragmented across ethics, metaphysics, and law without convergence.
6. **AI alignment — Gap: Value pluralism formalization.** While value pluralism is discussed philosophically, no formal framework exists for representing conflicting values in AI training.
7. **Process metaphysics for AI — Very new (2025–2026).** Whiteheadian frameworks for AI are nascent. Significant gap in engineering applications.
