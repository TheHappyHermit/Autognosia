# Wealthtech Business Model Archetypes — Condensed Reference

## The Five Archetypes

### A) Bootstrapped / Self-Funded
**Example:** Advyzon (founder Hailin Li, ex-Morningstar)
- $0 outside capital, $11.5M revenue (2024), 2,400+ firms
- 100% founder control, zero dilution
- Product-led growth, 50% engineering headcount
- Won Citi Wealth ($676B) contract — strongest validation of bootstrapped model
- Key constraint: must fund all growth organically
- **Lesson for WealthForge:** Capital-efficient first, raise later if at all

### B) Private Equity — Growth/Control
**Example:** Orion (TA Associates 2015, Genstar Capital 2020)
- ~$722M estimated revenue, $5.8T platform assets
- Roll-up vehicle: 8+ acquisitions (Redtail, HiddenLevers, BasisCode, etc.)
- Exit pressure within 5-7 year fund life
- UI fragmentation risk from acquisition stack
- TA reportedly seeking ~$2B exit (2025)

### C) Private Equity — Take-Private
**Examples:** Envestnet (Bain $4.5B, 2024), AssetMark (GTCR $2.7B, 2024)
- Public companies taken private for restructuring/transformation
- Organizational churn: CEO transitions, asset divestitures, 5-year plans
- Customer uncertainty during transition periods
- **Envestnet cautionary tale:** Yodlee acquired $590-660M (2015), sold at loss (2025); multiple CEO transitions; declining market relevance despite $6.5T assets
- **GTCR vs. Bain:** GTCR's "Leaders Strategy" = acquire profitable platforms at reasonable multiples; Bain = pay premium for transformation story

### D) Venture Capital — High-Growth, Pre-Profitable
**Examples:** Addepar ($759M, $3.25B), Altruist ($600M, $1.9B), Wealth.com ($95M+, ~$500M), TIFIN ($178M+, $842M)
- High cash burn, pressure to scale, dilution for founders
- Addepar: 6 share classes with liquidation preferences; $230M Series G = tender offer (liquidity event, not growth capital)
- Altruist: custodian model is capital-intensive; unprofitable despite $600M raised
- Wealth.com: best-positioned with strategic investors (Schwab, GV, Citi) providing distribution
- Exit paths: IPO or acquisition within VC fund timeline

### E) Domain-First / Practitioner-Incubated
**Examples:** Eton Solutions (spun out of Eton Advisors multi-family office), Canoe Intelligence (incubated in Portage Partners SFO)
- Origin: internal tools built by practitioners solving their own operational pain points
- Spun out as commercial products after reaching internal maturity
- Deep domain expertise embedded in product DNA: "built by X for X"
- Capital-efficient early years: Canoe reached $8.4M revenue with $0 external funding, 9 engineers serving 500+ firms
- Later-stage institutional capital: Canoe $61M total, Eton $58M Series C after reaching $1.3T+ AUM
- High customer retention and PMF (Eton 960+ families, Canoe 500+ firms)
- Stronger workflow understanding than tech-first platforms, but potentially narrower initial scope
- Revenue per employee often very high (Canoe: 180 employees, $11T+ AUA — ~$47K rev/employee, but extreme capital efficiency per dollar raised)
- **Key differentiator:** product-market fit is proven before any institutional capital is taken
- **Risk:** may lack tech-forward DNA (UI polish, API-first architecture) compared to VC-native competitors

**Sub-pattern: B2B-to-DTC expansion**
- Eton Solutions launched EtonAlpha ($25K-$125K/yr) as a direct-to-consumer offering for UHNW individuals ($25M-$500M net worth)
- Takes institutional-grade B2B technology and offers it directly to end-consumers bypassing advisors
- Serving the "missing middle" — 2.3M global HNWIs who need FO-grade tools but can't afford a full FO
- Requires concierge onboarding and consumer-grade UX, different from the core B2B product
- First-mover advantage if practitioner-incubated platform has the institutional credibility

**Sub-pattern: Practitioner-incubated as a fundraising narrative**
- "Built by X for X" origin story creates credible product ethos for institutional buyers
- Eton: "Built by a family office for family offices" — validated by 960+ families, $1.3T+ AUM
- Canoe: "Incubated in an SFO" — gave them 10 years of alts-specific ML training data advantage
- This narrative commands trust in RFPs and enterprise sales where technology buyers are also domain practitioners

## Strategic Lessons for WealthForge

1. **Advyzon path is replicable but slow** — 12 years to $11.5M, 14 years to Citi win
2. **Avoid custodian model** — Altruist's model requires massive regulatory capital
3. **Strategic investors > financial investors** — Wealth.com's Series B (Schwab, GV, Citi) provides distribution, not just capital
4. **PE exit pressure creates acquisition opportunities** — Unstable platforms create dissatisfied customers
5. **Revenue per employee target: $200K-$300K** — Achievable with unified codebase
6. **Domain-first narrative is a real enterprise sales asset** — "Built by financial planners for financial planners" could be WealthForge's origin story differentiator against tech-first competitors. Institutional buyers trust practitioner-built tools more than VC-built tools for compliance-critical software.
7. **B2B-to-DTC expansion is viable but risky** — Serving end-consumers requires different UX, support model, and regulatory posture. EtonAlpha's $25K-$125K/yr pricing validates the "virtual family office" as a DTC category.
