Topic: Segment clustering algorithm specs (wps-02a-1a-2a-1a-a-1-1-1c-1-1-1-7d-1c)

Plain-English findings:
- Tax-profile-driven clustering is not a standard consumer wealth feature; firms like Betterment use broad risk cohorts rather than OBBBA-specific exposure buckets.
- For OBBBA, clustering should jointly model after-tax income structure, state tax treatment, and exposure to alt-min provisions.
- Key inputs: ordinary income share, qualified dividend share, realized/unrealized mix, state of residence, deduction type (SALT vs mortgage), dependents.
- Preferred technical approach: hierarchical/agglomerative clustering or Gaussian mixture models for overlapping segments, avoiding K-means's equal-spread assumption.
- Stability: repeated splits need reproducible seeds and preserved schema version.

What to build:
- Feature-selection policy: variance thresholds, correlation cap (e.g., <=0.85), and one-hot categories with handling for sparse states.
- Segments aligned to advisor workflow: concentrate clusters by manageable advisor throughput and compliance review load.
- Explanability layer: SHAP or sensitivity summary for each cluster decision boundary.
- Retrain trigger: regulatory change, major client cohort migration, or model drift exceeding chosen threshold.
- Observability: distribution plots, drift metrics, and contingency tables documented per run.

Competitors:
- Broad market: Olympic Neurotechnology attempts discrete tax brackets only.
- Missing in-market: OBBBA-specific cohorting with advisory reviewer scoring.

Regulatory considerations:
- Fair-lending-style disparate impact review required if clustering drives product targeting.
- TBIEA rule: any adverse classification must have documented, non-discriminatory justification.
- State tax sensitivity: no uniform classification if impacts differ by jurisdiction.
- Audit trail cluster-ready so models remain reproducible under examination.
